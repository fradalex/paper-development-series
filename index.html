The idea

A place to think
out loud.

Research gets stronger when we share it before it feels finished.

The Paper Development Series brings together early-career researchers to discuss work in progress. We focus on innovation, science and technology, while welcoming research on other topics too.

There are two ways to share work: a regular paper discussion and, on some months, an additional session for ideas at an earlier stage.

01 / Monthly seminar

Paper discussion

The second Wednesday of every month

One researcher presents a paper in progress or a first draft. The group discusses the work in depth and offers constructive feedback to help refine the draft.

02 / Occasional second session

Idea incubation

Held in some months, alongside the main session

Several early-career researchers share very preliminary research ideas. Short presentations invite feedback, connect researchers with related interests and lay the groundwork for collaboration.

For speakers

Preparing a
discussion.

The structure of the conversation.

For either format, we encourage you to prepare a set of slides. Begin with a two-minute introduction of yourself and your broader research interests; include it within your presentation time. End with one or two questions on which feedback would help you most.

01 / Paper discussion

One paper, discussed in depth.

One speaker · one-hour session

35–40 minPaper presentation
20–25 minQuestions and discussion

Make your contribution clear, then put the emphasis on the empirical analysis or other original part of the paper. Give enough context for the audience to engage with your choices and offer constructive feedback.

02 / Idea incubation

Three ideas, one conversation.

Ideally three speakers · 20 minutes each

10–15 minIdea presentation
5–10 minDiscussion and suggestions

Focus on the research question, why it matters and how you plan to tackle it. Where relevant, sketch your empirical strategy and highlight the decisions on which early feedback would be most useful.
